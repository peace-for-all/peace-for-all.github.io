Case study · Active development

Earned

A personal Android tool exploring how progress can feel visible without becoming surveillance or a game you have to serve.

For
A person managing everyday commitments
Shape
Personal Android tool
Priority
Calm feedback

The nuisance

Useful routines often have delayed rewards. Most habit tools answer with streaks, scores, and reminders, which can make an ordinary life feel like a failing dashboard.

What became clear

The initial thought was to make effort measurable. The harder discovery was that measurement can distort the thing it is meant to support. The product needed to acknowledge completed work without punishing pauses or pretending that unlike tasks are interchangeable.

Decisions

The design keeps the feedback personal and legible. It favors deliberate entries, reversible choices, and a small number of states over engagement loops. The Android implementation is being shaped around local-first storage and a short path from action to reflection.

What works now

The core daily flow and local data model are usable in development. The tool can represent work and return a restrained view of progress without accounts or social comparison.

Status and limits

Earned is actively developed for personal use. Its language, model, and interaction rhythm are still being tested; it is not presented as a finished product or a validated behavior-change method.

Technology, where it matters

A native Android implementation allows offline use and keeps the interaction close to the device where the routine is recorded. Further technical detail will be published when the design stabilizes.

There is no public build or repository yet. Links will be added only after a release and source review.
