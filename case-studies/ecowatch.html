Case study · Prototype

EcoWatch

Field observations should survive patchy networks, tired hands, and the interval between noticing something and returning to a desk.

For
People recording environmental observations
Shape
Offline-first prototype
Core constraint
No dependable connection

The nuisance

Field notes can begin in paper, photos, and memory, then require reconstruction later. A connected form may be neat in an office and unusable at the moment an observation matters.

What became clear

The first assumption was that a digital form would remove transcription. The awkward discovery was that a form can simply move the burden into the field. Speed, incomplete information, correction, and trust in saved state mattered more than tidy completeness.

Decisions

The prototype treats local capture as the primary path rather than a degraded fallback. Draft observations remain editable, sync state is meant to be visible, and the data model distinguishes what was observed from later interpretation.

What works now

The prototype demonstrates local observation capture, persistence, and review across the core field workflow. It is useful for testing the information model and interaction sequence.

Status and limits

EcoWatch is a prototype, not a deployed monitoring service. Multi-device synchronization, operational security, long-term data governance, and use under a real field program remain unresolved and would require work with practitioners.

Technology, where it matters

The Android and local-storage choices exist to make capture reliable away from a network. The important technical question is not the library list but how conflicts, provenance, and failed transfers become understandable.

There is no public build or source link. The case study intentionally makes no environmental outcome claim.
